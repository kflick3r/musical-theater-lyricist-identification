# Project Proposal: Identifying Musical Theater Lyricists Using NLP and Stylometry

Musical theater lyricists each have their own writing styles, from the vocabulary they use to the way they structure their lyrics. This project will explore whether natural language processing (NLP) models can identify a lyricist based only on their song lyrics. I plan to use songs from musicals written by Oscar Hammerstein II, Stephen Sondheim, Stephen Schwartz, and Jason Robert Brown to train and compare two different text classification approaches.  The first will use TF-IDF with Logistic Regression, while the second will use embeddings from a pretrained Transformer model with Logistic Regression. Before training the models, I'll need to make sure I can find enough usable song lyrics from multiple musicals for each lyricist. I'm interested in seeing whether the models can recognize patterns in each lyricist's writing and whether using embeddings makes a difference in how accurately they can identify the writer. If time and the amount of available data allows, I'd also like to try fine-tuning a pretrained Transformer model, such as BERT, to see how it compares to the other two approaches. 

To evaluate the models, I'll compare their accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-scores. I also want to test the models in two different ways. First, I'll randomly split the songs into training and testing sets. Then, I'll try a more challenging approach by testing the models on songs from musicals that weren't included in the training data. I'm especially interested in whether the models can recognize a lyricist's writing style across different musicals or if they're mostly learning vocabulary and themes from specific shows. I'll also look at which lyricists the models confuse most often and try to understand why. Overall, I want to see how well NLP techniques can recognize individual writing styles within musical theater.
